# Independent review of the ten longest proofs

The lengths use the compiled declaration ranges of the measured proof revision. [Source locations](../data.md#ten-longest-proof-bodies).

| Declaration | Body code lines | Obligations reviewed |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `Paper.thm_onecut` | 275 | A single program works uniformly at the chosen scale. The proof uses separate estimates for finite-code evaluator selection, the common role alphabet, and the universal machine block budget. The remaining code makes the bit width, piece divisors, program construction, and explicit size and time bounds visible. |
| `Paper.prop_pieces` | 233 | Builds the actual PieceCut from a thin tree: choose an injective symbol coding, choose K and packet size from v and T, apply pieces_numbers and pieces_gain, then construct the table program using bounds on node counts, depth and inputs. A single hWC absorbs the logarithmic factor in each of two output bounds. One bound is for boundary units times v^300 and the other includes *all* units times k. They have different additive terms and use piece_cut and piece_all. The final record also supplies block, role, outcome, program and exact value clauses. The two final boundary and all-unit bounds use explicit arithmetic, so the theorem elaborates at the default heartbeat limit. |
| `Paper.thm_laws_over_field` | 218 | Transforms the price inequality into a finite family of laws. The two norm weights are different (unordered non-diagonal pairs versus neighbours). The proof chooses one q for every admissible load, embeds each index into bit strings of one common length, fills Law including connectedness, and proves both slot coverage and a dual norm bound. Nonemptiness of an index is deduced from the table identity. The two final estimates have distinct inputs, rounding and directions. It would be possible to present them as separate lemmas but their hypotheses include the previously chosen family and bounds. |
| `NS1.interp` | 216 | Induction on evaluation derivations. The shared decoder gives operands and opcode for each branch. Quotation also permits a malformed opcode to decode as a literal. Sequencing two child executions, conditional output and the run constructor require different alignment of bills, trees and lists; multiplication consumes the path appended to a bill. Distinct cases exercise real encoding semantics. The bound on the height of a tree after decoding recurs as one line, obtained directly from Bt.ht_decAll and membership in simS. |
| `Paper.thm_cut_iterate` | 178 | Distinguishes stopping with finalCut from cutting by v^63, recursing with new time/size and composing the two programs. Natural-number division and multiplication positivity are explicit; cut_div_mul_le handles the recurrent cancellation. In the substantial time estimate the four parts bound the first cut, recursive runtime, piece overhead and constant overhead. Size and program-size estimates follow different recurrences. The remaining time estimates could be given a private theorem for exposition without removing their arithmetic content. |
| `Paper.exists_thinTrees` | 174 | Assembles the ThinTrees record from a tower, placement, and program. tree_load_bounds computes common per-node / total load bounds; tree_computed supplies the program and calculation witnesses. The record also needs stage, role membership, agreement of old/new indexings, positions, copy counts and the size of the computed program. Examined the case split on relay vs law and the distinct final bounds; substituting one for the other changes the record fields. |
| `Nc.Grp.ex_ins` | 170 | Five actual instruction implementations (block, input, two lookups, lookup/literal, indexed role) share a switch but have different semantics, encodings, and running-time arguments. The five branches share a cost-distribution estimate, and two also share the trie lookup size proof. Focused Lean elaboration succeeded. Remaining two equality cases compare against a second lookup or a literal extracted from different stack positions. |
| `Nc.Tow.load_start_le` | 158 | Separates initial units on a first-stage edge from relays that land there. Different target constructors exclude all other contributions. A common sgl bound handles the Boolean end of a relay, then the path step-count theorem bounds remaining occurrences including orientation. The nested sums are over distinct endpoints, routes and path steps. |
| `Paper.Staged.piece_prog` | 150 | The ancestor relation, both subtree sums, the heavy/head predicates, iterated top pointers, the rank, and the final piece index are separate stages. Later stages consume subset proofs and stored circuit-size witnesses. |
| `Nc.Cut.sum_segs` | 148 | A finite-sum bijection for nonzero weights reads off blocks, uses uniqueness of the simultaneous row and column refinement of the staircase matrix, then reconstructs written and read strings, checks the bounds, and matches the weights. |

Two further proofs reviewed in the network modules:

- `Paper.thm_tree_moves`. Inducts over move prefixes; unused future outcome entries have a default value. The successor reindexes the sum by updating outcomes at one move. It proves agreement of previous good moves by causality, updates the seen set, current set and table, applies the single-move identity, and gives both inverse laws of the outcome reindexing. The last step identifies the whole prefix and factors the product by party. Those are distinct equalities of dependent data.
- `Nc.G.sum_pos`. Starts from a single sum over row data, counts, contents and the two directed lists of segments. The sum_bij_ne_zero proof constructs counts and contents from nonzero segment data, reconstructs a segment matrix from agreeing rows and columns for the reverse direction, and proves preservation of the weight. Nonvanishing is used substantively to bound lengths; a bijection of all possible raw tuples would be false.
